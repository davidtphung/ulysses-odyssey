Ulysses Creative direction study

A point of view

creative design direction

How the brand should look, sound, and feel as the fleet grows. Written plainly, and grounded in the site that already exists.

Ulysses already has a real point of view. The site is a deep navy ocean with one electric blue signal running through it. The headline reads in lowercase, calm and sure: "we steward the oceans." Nothing shouts. The water and the work do the talking.

So the question is not how to add more. It is how to grow the depth while keeping the silence. This is the direction I would hold from day one.

01 . What the brand already gets right

A strong point of view, built from very little.

The palette is the ocean at night. Deep navy fills the screen, white carries the words, and one electric blue stands in for the live signal. Color is rationed, so the blue actually means something when it appears.

The headlines run lowercase and human. "we steward the oceans" is the entire thesis in four words. The voice underneath it is confident and technical, the language of an operator, not a marketer.

Most of all, the brand sells a system. One vehicle, infinite possibilities. Mako works the water column, Kraken handles launch and recovery and recharge, Leviathan is on the horizon. This is a brand that already thinks in fleets. My job is to make every surface feel as engineered as the vehicles.

02 . The problem

One brand, talking to very different rooms.

Ulysses speaks to climate science, to defense, and to energy, often in the same week. Each of those rooms wants its own brochure, its own tone, its own promises. The risk is that the brand fractures into three voices and loses the single line that makes it Ulysses.

There is a second pressure. As the fleet and the team grow, more pages and more words arrive, each one reasonable on its own. Restraint dies by a thousand sensible additions if no one is guarding it.

The work is to hold one identity across the Atlantic and the Pacific, across science and security, all from one platform and one voice.

03 . The direction

Five principles guide everything I would make.

One live signal. The electric blue is telemetry, not decoration. Reserve it for things that are genuinely live: a vehicle on station, a reading coming back, a mission under way. When everything glows, nothing reads as alive.

The ocean at night. Deep navy is the canvas, and most of the screen stays dark and quiet. Light arrives only where the work is. That restraint is exactly what makes the data feel real.

Lowercase for the creed, mono for the proof. Keep the poetic lines lowercase and human, the way the homepage already does. Keep specs, telemetry, and labels in mono so the numbers read as measured. Two registers, one brand.

Sell the system, not the boat. Every hero answers "one vehicle, infinite possibilities" before it shows a single part. Mako, Kraken, and Leviathan are characters in one fleet, never three separate products.

Proof at depth. Show the real numbers, named plainly. 72 hours on station, 5000 feet down, 200 pounds of payload. Persistent maritime capability is a claim you earn with figures, not adjectives.

04 . The system

The tactical detail, kept short.

Type

A geometric sans for headlines, often set lowercase. A neutral sans for body. A mono for specs and telemetry. The live site reads as a geometric sans, and this study uses Space Grotesk, Inter, and Space Mono as open stand-ins.

Color

Deep navy #001E38 as the base. Electric blue #0A70FC for live signal. A cooler cyan #049FF3 for secondary data. White for text. The blue is rationed, never sprinkled.

Space

Dark, wide, and patient. Most of the canvas stays empty water. Content surfaces in focused pools of light rather than filling the frame.

Motion

Slow currents and live readouts. Things drift and pulse like instruments, they do not bounce. If a motion is not telemetry, it is decoration, and it goes.

Voice

Confident and technical, with room for one poetic line. Lowercase for the creed, precise for the spec. No hype, because the capability is the flex.

05 . How it shows up

Same brand, five surfaces.

Web

The marketing site stays a dark, quiet bridge. Depth lives in vehicle and mission pages that read like telemetry, not brochures.

Film

Shot at sea and subsea. Long takes, sonar and water for sound, and electric blue only where the system is working.

Defense and science

The same brand, tuned for the room. Clear specs, honest limits, dual domain capability stated plainly. Seriousness reads as credibility.

Recruiting

Engineers trust restraint. A careers page that respects attention will pull the people who build hard things at depth.

Social

One clip from a real mission, one mono line of data, posted only when there is something true to report.

06 . The first 90 days

Prove the system by flying it, not by presenting it.

Write down the rules the site already follows by instinct, then turn them into a real system. Most of the early work is naming what is already true.

Then ship three things. A short brand guide. One refreshed vehicle page that reads like a console. One mission film cut from real footage. Small, finished, and live beats large and theoretical every time.

07 . The moodboard

Artifacts and historical inspirations.

Six reference artifacts that set the tone, each a self contained 1000 by 1000 graphic in the Ulysses palette. Voyagers and defenders, the US Navy watch, the fleet, and the mission. Tap any tile to open it full size.

In one line

the vehicles stay out for days, see what no one has seen, and report only what is real. the brand should run on the same power.

Keep it dark, keep it precise, and let one blue signal carry the life.
